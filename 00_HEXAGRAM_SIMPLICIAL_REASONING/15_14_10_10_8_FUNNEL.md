## 0. PURPOSE

The previous file defined how the full reasoning architecture should be tested on an external benchmark.

This file isolates one internal architectural hypothesis:

```text
14 -> 10 -> 10 -> 8
```

The critical rule is:

> **14 -> 10 -> 10 -> 8 is not treated as a universal law. It is treated as a candidate information funnel whose usefulness must be measured against alternative layer widths and alternative stage semantics.**

This file defines:

```text
what each stage could mean
what must be preserved
what may be compressed
how the stages connect
which baselines must compete
what would falsify the hypothesis
```

---

# 1. ORIGIN OF THE SEQUENCE

The sequence:

```text
14
10
10
8
```

appears as a nested structural count in Sri Yantra / Sri Chakra descriptions.

For this project, that historical/geometric fact is not enough to justify an AI architecture.

Therefore separate:

```text
GEOMETRIC SOURCE
```

from:

```text
COMPUTATIONAL HYPOTHESIS
```

The architecture must stand or fall by performance.

---

# 2. NO NUMEROLOGICAL CLAIM

This project does not claim:

```text
14 is universally the correct input width
10 is universally the correct hidden width
8 is universally the correct output width
```

Nor does it claim:

```text
nature computes through 14 -> 10 -> 10 -> 8
```

The research question is narrower:

> Can a four-stage bottleneck with these widths provide a useful balance between representation capacity, interpretability, and compression?

# 4. WHY A FUNNEL

A funnel is intended to reduce active representation while preserving task-relevant structure.

General objective:

```text
large descriptive state
      |
      v
structured reduction
      |
      v
smaller actionable state
```

This is a standard architectural idea.

The unusual part being tested here is:

```text
the proposed widths
and
the proposed semantics of the four stages
```

# 80. PARTICIPATION RATIO

For eigenvalues or singular values:

```math
\lambda_i
```

a common effective-rank style quantity is:

```math
D_{\mathrm{PR}}
=
\frac{
(\sum_i \lambda_i)^2
}{
\sum_i \lambda_i^2
}
```

This can estimate how many modes are effectively active.

Use carefully according to the matrix being analyzed.

---

```math

}{
```

Very high utilization near one may indicate a tight bottleneck.
